What it does

Control that goes app by app, hour by hour.

Do Not Disturb silences everything at once. Turning notifications off destroys them. Filter sits between the two: precise rules, and a complete record of what they caught.

Schedule each app separately

Give an app an allow-window and it only gets through inside it. Give it a block-window and it goes quiet just for those hours. Minute precision, any weekdays, wrapping past midnight — 22:00 to 07:00 works exactly as you'd expect.

Mute one channel, keep the rest

Apps post on separate notification channels. Silence a group-chat channel and let direct messages through. Channels appear as soon as an app posts on one, so the list only ever shows what actually reaches you.

Muted, never missed

A muted notification is written to your history before it is dismissed. The status bar stays quiet; the message is still there when you're ready. Silence stops costing you information.

Block All — now, or on a timer

One switch mutes every app instantly. The same screen keeps its own set of windows, so "everything quiet 22:00 to 07:00" runs nightly without touching a single per-app rule.

Search everything you were sent

Your history is searchable across app name, title and body. Filter it down to All, Muted or Saved, swipe right to keep a notification forever, swipe left to bin it — with undo either way.

Locked to you

Notification history is personal, so app lock is free for everyone. Fingerprint, face or your device PIN gates the whole app at launch and every time you come back to it.

How it works

Four steps to a quieter phone.

Set it up once. After that Filter only does anything when a notification actually arrives — no polling, no background scanning, no daily maintenance from you.

Grant Notification Access

One system permission lets Filter read, log and dismiss notifications. Onboarding walks you to the right screen and checks the two smaller permissions that keep it running reliably.

Pick what interrupts you

Every installed app is listed and searchable. Flip a switch to mute one outright, or open it and mute individual channels instead.

Draw your windows

Name them the way you think about them — Sleep, Deep work, Weekend detox. Choose the days, or a specific date range for a holiday, and switch a window off whenever you don't want it.

Catch up on your terms

The Home tab shows what was just filtered out and a "blocked today" count. History holds the rest, searchable, until you decide what's worth keeping.

Questions

Good to know before you start.

Do I lose the notifications Filter mutes?

No. Filter writes a notification to your local history first and dismisses it second, so a muted notification is always readable afterwards — app, title, body, channel and timestamp included. That's the whole point: quiet without loss.

Why does Filter need Notification Access?

It's the only Android permission that lets an app see and dismiss notifications, so nothing works without it. Granting it is a deliberate, manual step in system settings — and what Filter does with it stays entirely on your device.

Will a muted notification still flash for a moment?

Sometimes, yes. Android delivers a notification before any app can act on it, so Filter cancels it right after it's posted. On some phones it may briefly appear or make a sound before it disappears. Every app in this category works this way — we'd rather say so than promise otherwise.

Why can't I see an app's channels yet?

Android doesn't let one app list another app's notification channels, so Filter learns them live: a channel shows up the first time that app posts a notification on it. Give it a day of normal use and the list fills itself in.

How long is my history kept?

Rows older than 30 days are purged automatically to keep the database small. Anything you swipe to save is exempt and stays until you delete it. Settings shows exactly how much space history is using and can wipe it in one action.

What's free and what's Pro?

Free covers unlimited per-app and per-channel muting, Block All, the Home feed, biometric app lock, every accent theme, and two schedule windows. Pro removes the window limit and unlocks the full searchable history, plus a cloud backup of the apps you block. It's available monthly, yearly, or as a one-time lifetime purchase through Google Play.

Does this replace Do Not Disturb?

It does a different job. Do Not Disturb is one global rule for the whole phone; Filter gives every app its own hours, keeps a record of what it silenced, and can still be overridden instantly with Block All. Many people run both.

Will it drain my battery?

It shouldn't. Filter never polls, never scans in the background and never sets alarms — your rules are held in memory and checked only when a notification arrives. Do allow it to skip battery optimisation, though: aggressive power managers on some phones can stop the listener from running.

Do I need an account?

No. Every free feature works without signing in. Google sign-in is optional — it links a Pro purchase to your Google account so it follows you to other devices and, with Pro, backs up the list of apps you've blocked so they're restored on a new phone.

Is any of my data uploaded?

Never your notifications. History, channels and schedules stay in a local database on your phone. If you choose to sign in with Google and have Pro, the list of apps you've blocked is backed up to your account so it comes back on a new phone. The app also sends anonymous usage statistics through Firebase Analytics. Details are in the Privacy Policy.
