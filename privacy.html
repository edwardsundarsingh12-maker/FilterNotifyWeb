Privacy Policy

The short version

Your notifications never leave your phone. Every notification Filter reads, every channel and schedule you create, and every entry in your history is stored in a database on your device and is never transmitted anywhere. Signing in is optional. If you do sign in with Google, we store your email, name, Pro status, purchase records and — with Pro — the list of apps you've blocked, so they can be restored on another device. The app also sends anonymous usage statistics through Firebase Analytics. There are no ads.

We think a notification reader is only trustworthy if what it reads stays with you. That is how Filter is built: the code that talks to the cloud handles only your account, your purchases and your list of blocked apps, and has no access to notification content.

Scope

This policy covers the Android application Filter: Schedule Notification (package com.filternotify.app, "Filter", "the app") and this website. It does not cover the other apps whose notifications Filter displays; those remain governed by their own privacy policies.

What Filter stores, and where

To do its job, Filter uses Android's notification listener service, which lets it see notifications posted by other apps. The following is written to a local database on your device:

  • Notification records — the posting app's package name and label, the notification title and body text, the notification channel, and the time it arrived, plus whether Filter muted it and whether you saved it.
  • Your rules — which apps and channels are muted, your named time windows with their hours, weekdays or date ranges, and the state of the Block All switch.
  • App settings — accent colour, launcher-badge preference, app-lock preference, and your entitlement status.

Notification records, channels, schedule windows and settings stay in the app's private storage. They are not uploaded, shared with anyone, or used to build a profile. We — the developer — cannot see any of it. The only exception is the optional backup of your blocked-app list, described next.

Optional account and cloud backup

You can use every free feature without an account. Signing in with Google is optional and is offered in Settings → Account and when you buy Pro. Sign-in is handled by Google (Android Credential Manager and Firebase Authentication). When you sign in, the following is stored in Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore), readable only by your own account:

  • Profile — your Google email address, display name, and when the account was created.
  • Pro status — whether you have Pro and until when, so it can be restored on your other devices.
  • Purchase records — product, plan, order ID, purchase time and renewal state of each Google Play purchase. The purchase token is never stored.
  • Blocked apps (Pro only) — for each app you block: its package name, its display name, and when it was blocked or unblocked. This lets your blocked apps come back automatically when you sign in on a new phone.

Never uploaded: notification titles, text or any other notification content, your history, saved notifications, notification channels, schedule windows, Block All settings, or app settings.

You can sign out at any time in Settings. Firebase is operated by Google; see the Firebase privacy information.

Analytics

Filter includes Google Analytics for Firebase to understand, in aggregate, how many people use the app and on what devices. It collects the standard automatic events — such as first open, app sessions and app updates — along with device model, Android version, app version, approximate country and an app-instance identifier. Filter does not log any custom events, and never sends notification content, the apps you block, or anything you type. Analytics data is processed by Google under the Google Privacy Policy.

What we never do

  • Never upload, read or analyse the content of your notifications on any server.
  • No advertising, no ad SDKs, no third-party marketing tools.
  • No selling or renting of personal information, and no sharing except with the service providers named on this page.
  • Never require an account to use the free app.

Permissions and why each one exists

PermissionWhy Filter asks for it
Notification Access
BIND_NOTIFICATION_LISTENER_SERVICE		 The core permission. It lets Filter read incoming notifications, save them to your local history and dismiss the ones your rules mute. Nothing in the app works without it, and it must be granted by you in system settings.
Query all packages
QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES		 Lists the apps installed on your device so that any of them can appear in the Apps tab and be muted or scheduled. The list is only rendered on screen; it is never uploaded.
Post notifications
POST_NOTIFICATIONS		 Android 13 and later. Lets Filter show its own quiet status notification so you always know when filtering is active.
Biometrics
USE_BIOMETRIC		 Optional app lock. Authentication is handled entirely by Android; Filter receives only a success or failure result and never sees your fingerprint or face data.
Billing
com.android.vending.BILLING		 Lets you buy and restore Pro through Google Play.
Internet
INTERNET		 Used for Google Play Billing, optional Google sign-in and cloud backup, Firebase Analytics, and to open this website inside the app. It is never used to transmit notification content.
Battery-optimisation exemption Optional but recommended. Some manufacturers aggressively stop background services; the exemption keeps the notification listener alive so your rules keep working.

Purchases

Pro is sold through Google Play Billing. Payment details are handled entirely by Google — Filter never sees your card or billing address. The app receives a purchase confirmation that tells it whether an entitlement is active. Buying Pro asks you to sign in with Google so the purchase can be restored on your other devices; the purchase record stored with your account is listed above. Google's handling of that transaction is governed by the Google Privacy Policy.

Retention and deletion

  • History entries older than 30 days are deleted automatically.
  • Entries you save are exempt from that purge and stay until you delete them.
  • Settings → Storage shows how much space history is using and can erase all of it immediately.
  • Uninstalling Filter removes the local database, including every rule, window and saved notification. If you signed in, your profile, purchase records and blocked-app backup stay with your account until you ask us to delete them (see Your rights).

Security

Filter's data lives in Android's private per-app storage, which other apps cannot read. Because notification history can be sensitive, the app also offers a biometric lock that gates the entire app at launch and whenever you return to it — free on every tier. As with any on-device data, the strongest protection remains a secure device lock screen and up-to-date system software.

Children

Filter is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children; if you believe a child has signed in, contact us and we will delete that account's data. If a child uses a device with Filter installed, the guidance in this policy applies unchanged.

Your rights and choices

Privacy laws such as the GDPR and CCPA grant rights of access, correction, deletion and portability over personal data held by a company. If you never sign in, we hold no record about you beyond anonymous analytics. If you did sign in, email us from that Google account and we will provide or delete your profile, purchase records and blocked-app backup. On your device you have full control:

  • Revoke Notification Access at any time in Android settings — filtering stops immediately.
  • Clear your history from Settings → Storage.
  • Delete individual notifications by swiping them away.
  • Sign out from Settings → Account to stop cloud backup.
  • Uninstall the app to remove everything stored on the device.

If you contact us by email, we will hold your message only as long as needed to answer it.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices ever change, this page will be updated and the "last updated" date above will change with it. Material changes will also be described in the release notes on the What's New page. Continuing to use Filter after an update means you accept the revised policy.
