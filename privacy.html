Privacy Policy

Filter reads your notifications so it can mute and remember them. This page explains exactly what that means, and why none of it reaches us.

The short version

Filter has no servers, no accounts and no analytics. Every notification it reads, every rule you create and every entry in your history is stored in a single database on your phone and is never transmitted anywhere. Uninstall the app and all of it is gone.

We think a notification reader is only trustworthy if it is architecturally incapable of leaking what it sees. That is how Filter is built — there is no network code that could send your notification content anywhere, because there is nowhere for it to go.

Scope

This policy covers the Android application Filter: Schedule Notification (package com.filternotify.app, "Filter", "the app") and this website. It does not cover the other apps whose notifications Filter displays; those remain governed by their own privacy policies.

What Filter stores, and where

To do its job, Filter uses Android's notification listener service, which lets it see notifications posted by other apps. The following is written to a local database on your device:

  • Notification records — the posting app's package name and label, the notification title and body text, the notification channel, and the time it arrived, plus whether Filter muted it and whether you saved it.
  • Your rules — which apps and channels are muted, your named time windows with their hours, weekdays or date ranges, and the state of the Block All switch.
  • App settings — accent colour, launcher-badge preference, app-lock preference, and your entitlement status.

This data stays in the app's private storage. It is not uploaded, backed up to a service of ours, shared with anyone, or used to build a profile. We — the developer — cannot see any of it, and neither can anyone else without physical access to your unlocked device.

What we never do

  • No account, sign-in, email capture or user identifier of any kind.
  • No cloud storage, no sync between devices, no remote backup.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting SDKs or usage tracking.
  • No advertising, no ad identifiers, no third-party marketing SDKs.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal information — there is none to share.

Permissions and why each one exists

PermissionWhy Filter asks for it
Notification Access
BIND_NOTIFICATION_LISTENER_SERVICE		 The core permission. It lets Filter read incoming notifications, save them to your local history and dismiss the ones your rules mute. Nothing in the app works without it, and it must be granted by you in system settings.
Query all packages
QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES		 Lists the apps installed on your device so that any of them can appear in the Apps tab and be muted or scheduled. The list is only rendered on screen; it is never uploaded.
Post notifications
POST_NOTIFICATIONS		 Android 13 and later. Lets Filter show its own quiet status notification so you always know when filtering is active.
Biometrics
USE_BIOMETRIC		 Optional app lock. Authentication is handled entirely by Android; Filter receives only a success or failure result and never sees your fingerprint or face data.
Billing
com.android.vending.BILLING		 Lets you buy and restore Pro through Google Play.
Internet
INTERNET		 Used only for Google Play Billing and to open this privacy policy, the terms and the about page inside the app. It is never used to transmit notification content.
Battery-optimisation exemption Optional but recommended. Some manufacturers aggressively stop background services; the exemption keeps the notification listener alive so your rules keep working.

Purchases

Pro is sold through Google Play Billing. Payment details are handled entirely by Google — Filter never sees your card, address or Google account. The app receives only a purchase token that tells it whether an entitlement is active, and stores that locally so Pro features stay unlocked. Google's handling of that transaction is governed by the Google Privacy Policy.

Retention and deletion

  • History entries older than 30 days are deleted automatically.
  • Entries you save are exempt from that purge and stay until you delete them.
  • Settings → Storage shows how much space history is using and can erase all of it immediately.
  • Uninstalling Filter removes the entire database, including every rule, window and saved notification. There is no backup to restore from, and nothing survives elsewhere.

Security

Filter's data lives in Android's private per-app storage, which other apps cannot read. Because notification history can be sensitive, the app also offers a biometric lock that gates the entire app at launch and whenever you return to it — free on every tier. As with any on-device data, the strongest protection remains a secure device lock screen and up-to-date system software.

Children

Filter is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children under 13. It collects no personal information from anyone, so it holds none from children either. If a child uses a device with Filter installed, the guidance in this policy applies unchanged.

Your rights and choices

Privacy laws such as the GDPR and CCPA grant rights of access, correction, deletion and portability over personal data held by a company. Because Filter transmits nothing and we hold no data about you, there is no company-held record to access or erase. You exercise full control directly on your device:

  • Revoke Notification Access at any time in Android settings — filtering stops immediately.
  • Clear your history from Settings → Storage.
  • Delete individual notifications by swiping them away.
  • Uninstall the app to remove everything.

If you contact us by email, we will hold your message only as long as needed to answer it.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices ever change, this page will be updated and the "last updated" date above will change with it. Material changes will also be described in the release notes on the What's New page. Continuing to use Filter after an update means you accept the revised policy.
