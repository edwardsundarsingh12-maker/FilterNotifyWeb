6. What the app can and cannot guarantee

Filter works within the limits Android places on every app in its category. So that you are never misled, those limits are stated plainly:

  • Muting happens after delivery. Android hands a notification to the system before any app can act on it. Filter saves it, then dismisses it — so on some devices a muted notification may briefly appear or make a sound before it disappears. Filter does not prevent notifications from ever being posted.
  • Notification Access is required. If you never grant it, or revoke it later, no rule can be enforced and nothing will be logged.
  • Manufacturer power management can stop the listener. Some devices aggressively kill background services. Filter asks for a battery-optimisation exemption, but cannot guarantee the system will keep it running.
  • Channels are learned, not listed. Android does not let one app enumerate another app's notification channels, so a channel only becomes available after that app has posted on it at least once.
  • History is not a backup. Entries older than 30 days are purged automatically unless saved, and history is never exported or copied to the cloud. Cloud backup covers only the list of apps you block. Uninstalling deletes everything stored on the device.

Do not rely on Filter as the sole safeguard for notifications you cannot afford to miss, such as medical, safety or emergency alerts.
